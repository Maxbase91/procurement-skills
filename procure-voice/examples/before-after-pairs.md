# Before / After: procure-voice in action

A collection of rewrite pairs across common procurement contexts. Use as a reference when applying the skill.

---

## Board update

### Before
> "Following extensive engagement with key stakeholders across the business, the procurement function has identified a number of strategic initiatives that, if successfully executed, have the potential to deliver meaningful savings while simultaneously enhancing operational resilience and supplier-side risk posture. The most material opportunity relates to the consolidation of our current SaaS supplier base, where significant fragmentation has been observed."

### After
> "We're running 61 SaaS tools across the company. Probably need 8 strategic ones plus Microsoft 365. Consolidation saves €120–180k a year. Trade-off: 3-month transition pain in IT. Plan: SaaS inventory in Q1, decisions in Q2, switches in Q3."

**What changed**: Buried lede moved up. Numbers added. Hedging cut. Trade-off named explicitly. Plan with quarters.

## RFP scoring justification

### Before
> "Vendor A demonstrated a robust set of capabilities across the functional requirements, with particular strength in the areas of integration and reporting, although their commercial response was somewhat above the median of submissions received."

### After
> "Vendor A's integrations and reporting are strong — best of the five. Their price is the second-highest at €112k/year vs median €98k. The functionality gap justifies €5-10k more; not €14k."

**What changed**: Vague praise replaced with specifics. Price compared to median. Recommendation embedded.

## Internal email — request to issue an RFP

### Before
> "Hi team, I hope this message finds you well. I wanted to reach out to flag that we have an opportunity to refresh our existing arrangements in the contact centre technology space, and I'd be keen to align on next steps and timelines. Happy to circle back if any of you have bandwidth to discuss."

### After
> "Our current ticketing tool contract ends 30 June. I want to issue an RFP in Feb, decide in April. Need 30 minutes from each of you (Sarah, James, Priya) next week to agree requirements. Calendar invites going out today."

**What changed**: Greeting trimmed. Vague "opportunity" replaced with deadline. Specific names. Concrete next action.

## Negotiation email to a supplier

### Before
> "Dear Mark, Thank you for sharing the renewal proposal. We've had the opportunity to review it internally and have a number of points we'd like to discuss in order to ensure mutual alignment going forward. We're hopeful that there's scope for us to find a mutually beneficial outcome."

### After
> "Hi Mark, thanks for the renewal terms. Three points to discuss before we can sign:
> 1. The 7% price increase. Our cap is 3% — same as last year.
> 2. The auto-renewal clause. We need 90 days notice, not 30.
> 3. The exit fee. €15k to export our own data isn't workable.
>
> Can we get on a call Thursday to work through these?"

**What changed**: Pleasantries minimal. Three concrete issues. Specific asks. Specific next action.

## Spend analysis executive summary

### Before
> "Our analysis of FY25 spend data has revealed a number of interesting insights, including a relatively high degree of supplier fragmentation in certain indirect categories and opportunities for enhanced category management going forward. We believe there is significant potential to drive value through a more strategic approach."

### After
> "Top 10 suppliers = 56% of spend. Tail = 327 suppliers and €1.1M. Three priorities for FY26:
> 1. AWS renegotiation — €280-420k savings, this quarter
> 2. SaaS consolidation (61 tools → 8) — €120-180k, full year
> 3. Office supplies tender (47 → 3) — €40-60k, Q1"

**What changed**: Generic insight replaced with quantified findings. Ranked actions with savings ranges and timing.

## Contract review finding

### Before
> "We have identified that the liability cap, as currently drafted, may not adequately protect our interests in the event of certain types of breach scenarios, and we would recommend that consideration be given to amending the relevant clause."

### After
> "The liability cap is 1× annual fees. Our standard is 12×. Push to 12×; accept 6× minimum. Data breach must be uncapped."

**What changed**: Hedging cut. Standard quoted as a number. Negotiation range stated. Critical exception called out.

## Supplier QBR / performance update

### Before
> "It is worth highlighting that the supplier has demonstrated a solid performance against the agreed SLAs over the course of the past quarter, although there have been certain instances where issues have emerged that may warrant further attention and dialogue."

### After
> "Acme hit 99.4% uptime — under their 99.5% SLA, so service credits apply (~€8k). Two P1 incidents, both resolved under SLA. Watch item: their EU support team turnover. New CSM is the third in 12 months."

**What changed**: Performance quantified. SLA breach called out with the credit number. Specific watch item named.

## Stakeholder pushback handling

### Before
> "Whilst I appreciate the perspective shared, I would gently push back on the suggestion that we should proceed with single-sourcing in this instance, as there are a number of considerations that I believe warrant further discussion."

### After
> "I'd argue against single-sourcing here. Three reasons:
> 1. We don't have a credible benchmark for the price.
> 2. The incumbent's renewal leverage is high already.
> 3. We've had two service issues in the last six months.
>
> Suggest a 3-vendor RFI as a compromise — quick, low effort, gives us options."

**What changed**: Apologetic preamble cut. Disagreement stated cleanly. Reasons numbered. Compromise offered.

## What stays formal

Some procurement writing should stay precise and somewhat formal, even with procure-voice applied:

- **Contract clauses themselves**: legal language has work to do; don't casualise it
- **Regulatory submissions**: follow the regulator's expected register
- **Audit responses**: precise, factual, traceable
- **Disciplinary or termination letters**: the formality is part of the protection

In these cases, procure-voice still applies to:
- Cutting unnecessary jargon
- Adding specifics
- Removing performative hedging
- Leading with the point

It does not apply to: making everything sound like a Slack message.
