# Category-specific RFP advice

Read this when generating an RFP. Include at least 3 relevant tips in the final output.

---

## SaaS / Software

**Pricing tricks to watch for**:
- "Starting from" pricing — force vendors into your pricing template with all-in 3-year TCO
- "Implementation included" with weasel asterisks — define scope of implementation in the RFP
- Per-user pricing with hidden floor — ask for "minimum committed users" explicitly
- Annual increases not capped — ask for cap (CPI or fixed %, max 5%)

**Things that are now mandatory in 2026 RFPs but often forgotten**:
- AI training on customer data: yes/no question with no wiggle room
- Sub-processor list (and approval rights for changes)
- Data residency commitment
- Exit assistance: data export format and timeframe
- Source code escrow if business-critical and vendor is < €50M revenue

**Where vendors lie**:
- Uptime SLAs ("99.99%") that exclude planned maintenance, force majeure, third-party providers
- "Enterprise-grade security" without certifications to back it up
- "AI-powered" — define what that actually means in the response

## Professional Services / Consulting

**Pricing tricks**:
- Day rates without role breakdown — force a rate card with experience levels
- "Senior consultant" titles meaning "partner discount needed to make it work"
- T&E "at cost" without cap — set a % of fees or absolute cap

**Watch the team**:
- Demand named team members in the response and contract them in
- Get CVs as appendix, including the project manager
- Bait-and-switch is the #1 consulting risk

**References**:
- Ask for 3 references in the same industry, similar engagement size
- Insist on speaking to them; don't accept written references only

---

## Hardware / Manufactured Goods

**TCO is everything**:
- Acquisition + delivery + installation + spares + service + decommission
- Energy consumption over expected life (especially for IT hardware)
- Compatibility with existing infrastructure

**Logistics**:
- Lead times — get worst case, not "typical"
- Where is it made? (sanctions, tariffs, supply chain risk)
- After-sales support: SLA, geography, language

---

## Agency / Creative Services

**Beat the pitch presentation problem**:
- Pitch teams are not delivery teams. Ask: "Who will actually work on this account day-to-day?"
- Demand the senior creative names you saw in the pitch be contractually committed
- Ask about agency turnover rate

**Pricing structure**:
- Retainer vs project — be explicit
- Out-of-scope process — how do change requests get priced?
- Media commissions — disclosed or hidden?

---

## Facilities / Services (Cleaning, Security, Catering)

**The race to the bottom**:
- Lowest price wins, then the service quality collapses. Build in service-quality scoring.
- Mystery shopper / audit rights
- Performance penalties (small, but they focus the mind)

**People matter here**:
- TUPE / acquired rights of existing workforce
- Pay rates for line staff (Living Wage commitments)
- Right to work checks

---

## Cross-category tips (always include at least one)

**Beware the demo effect**: Vendors are great at demos. Ask scripted questions during demos; force the same scenarios across all bidders.

**Reference check ≠ vendor-supplied reference list**: Find your own references through LinkedIn / industry contacts. Vendor references are pre-coached.

**The "what would you change" question**: Ask each vendor what they'd do differently if money were no object. Their answer reveals their actual opinion.

**Have a Plan B**: Always shortlist at least 2 viable vendors. Sole-source negotiations always go badly for the buyer.
